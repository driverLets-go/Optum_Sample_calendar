01 · Who Gets In, And How

How Every Capability Enters Your Community.

Every profile is sourced and screened through its identity before it's added to anything. That check runs first, ahead of any skill match or invitation, because a pool at real market scale (tens of thousands of relevant professionals across a market this size) is only as useful as it is relevant. Cast a wide net with no check on the way in, and reach turns into noise fast.

01
Name & Identity

Confirmed, not just collected. The person is who the profile says they are before anything else runs.

02
Project Exposure

What they were actually staffed on and worked inside, checked against how they describe it themselves.

03
Ownership

What they personally led or built, versus what they sat near. The gap a job title alone can't show.

04
In Their Own Words

Not a resume, which is trivial to produce with AI today. A short audio or video account, or something written directly by the person, so what's checked is their own record: what they did, where, with whom, and under what circumstances.

01

Fit & Context

What they actually work on today, in their own words, checked against the identity and exposure signals above before it goes anywhere. Nothing about this stage is a form to be filled in and forgotten. It's the first pass that decides which capability cluster a profile even belongs in, and it takes as long as it takes to get that right.

Signal Match · Identity & Exposure
02

Proof Of Work

A cluster-specific check against real work: a claims-adjudication or EDI systems walkthrough for Cloud & Infra, a short applied case for AI/ML, a portfolio review for Product & UX/CX, whatever that cluster actually runs day to day, in this business specifically. This is where ownership and circumstance get tested directly, not inferred from a job title.

Ownership Check · Cluster-Specific
03

Live Conversation

A conversation with someone who actually works in that domain, not HR, not a generalist recruiter. This is the human check on everything the first two stages surfaced, and it's also the first real WeiTA touchpoint the professional experiences.

Human Review · Domain-Led
This is deliberately a funnel, not a single gate. Each stage does one job, so no single yes/no decision carries the whole weight of who gets in. At the scale this community is meant to reach, that discipline is what keeps a very large pool from collapsing into a generic one: relevance is checked on the way in, not sorted out after the fact.

03 · The Cadence & Format Library

Calibrated To The Audience, Not Asserted.

Nobody in a recruiting function decides what shows up here, that's the actual point. Every format below is built or vetted by people who do this work day to day: engineers, architects, delivery leads, not a generic content or talent-acquisition desk that's never touched a claims pipeline or a FHIR integration and it shows the moment they try to write about one. That's also why the cadence stays tight rather than heavy, this audience is senior, largely passive, and tunes out fast from anything that reads like it was written by someone without the depth to have actually done the work. Six months are shown here, October through March, each one led by a different capability cluster, so the mix visibly rotates instead of repeating the same four formats on a loop. The capability isn't a label on top. It decides what gets discussed, who leads it, which format fits, and what the relationship can turn into.

Eight Formats, Rotating By Design

Not Eight Random Content Types, Eight Different Jobs

Each one recurs monthly, at feature level rather than as a frequency count, so no single month is carrying all its weight on one format.

    Every format above is written or produced by someone who actually does that work (an engineer, a delivery lead, a domain architect), never a content team working from a brief. That distinction isn't a nicety: research across large professional communities consistently finds only around one in ten members ever create anything and roughly one in twenty respond to a given post, and close to half of newcomers never send a second message once the first interaction reads as generic. Five formats (Publications, the Virtual Roundtable, the In-Person Meetup, the Hackathon and Speed Mentoring) carry a "How This Runs" note of their own; open it for the actual mechanism, not just the mockup.
    Four formats in the library come straight from community-engagement research: an async Leadership AMA (Metaview's highest-engagement documented format, run as one thread a month rather than a weekly feed), a repurposed-content Podcast (recorded sessions cut into audio, per RecruitBPM), a peer-nominated Recognition Spotlight (the single most common recognition format across every source researched, from Wikipedia's Barnstars to Wild Apricot's member spotlights), and Speed Mentoring: five-to-ten-minute round-robin pairings, closed with a "match card" so a strong pairing can continue past the session (a 2016 University of Hamburg study found mentoring relationships formed this way outlast ones formed through ordinary networking). Train & Deploy and Offer To Join are deliberately missing from the library. They're results, not formats, and they sit in section 04.
